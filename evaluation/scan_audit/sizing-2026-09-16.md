# Sizing the two defect classes the field audit found

The audit found a multi-word ideophone losing tone and a headword whose consonant
was read as its neighbour. Before repairing either, the size has to be known:
four records or four hundred decides whether this is a fix or a project. Both can
be screened from data already held, with the scan verifying a sample rather than
being read 40,000 times.

`tools/size_audit_candidates.py` runs both screens.

## Ideophone tone — 26 records, bounded

A multi-word ideophone's bracket carries one group per word (`kwakwara kwakwara
[LLL LLL]`). Of the **1,205** multi-word ideophones, **26** hold a pattern that
cannot cover every word.

That is the answer the audit needed: not four records and not four hundred, but
26 — a repair job with known scope. Examples:

| headword | words | stored pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kwakwara kwakwara` | 2 | `LLL` |
| `bve bve` | 2 | `HHH` |
| `kwa kwa kwa` | 3 | `L` |
| `kwangwandi kwangwandi` | 2 | `LLH` |

Caveat to carry into the repair: some ideophones may legitimately carry a single
group for the whole expression, so the 26 are candidates until their brackets are
read. `kwa kwa kwa` with `L` is the kind that could go either way.

## Headword consonants — 527 candidates, and not yet a count

**527** of 38,272 published headwords are one confusable character from the
dictionary text's spelling, using only pairs the audit has actually observed
confused (`b`/`h`, `v`/`w`, `d`/`dh`, `z`/`zv`, `s`/`sh`, `t`/`th`, `k`/`kh`).

This is a **candidate set, not a defect count**, and the screen's own output says
why: the pairs run both ways. `bvubvuva` is flagged against text `bvubvuwa`, and
`bvubvuwa` is flagged against text `bvubvuva`. The same for `chava`/`chawa`. When
both spellings exist on each side, some fraction of the 527 are legitimate pairs
of distinct words, or the text is the one that is wrong — and the screen cannot
tell which, because it compares two derived readings and neither is the page.

Only the scan settles it. A sample of the 527 read against the pages would say
what proportion are real; until then this number should not be quoted as a
defect count.

## A note on the instrument

Both screens reported zero on their first run, and both were wrong:

- the headword comparison replaced the *first* occurrence of a character, so a
  difference later in the word (`nzwaivhi` / `nzwaiwhi` differ at the fifth)
  never matched;
- the text index was built by a regex anchored to the line start, and the
  extraction holds two printed columns per line, so only the first column was
  indexed;
- and under both, `REPOSITORY_ROOT` resolved to `tools/` rather than the
  repository, so every path missed and an empty index was read as "no defects".

Each produced a confident zero for a defect that the scan had already shown to be
real. That is the fourth time this session a measurement of mine failed silently
and its output was nearly reported as a finding — after the wrong output
directory, the tool framing the wrong column, and the imprecise counts. **A
screen that returns zero should be checked against a case known to be positive
before it is believed**, and that is what caught this one.
